    How the ratings work

    Each player gets a game score from 23 stats: the box score, every shot with its expected-goal value, and what happened while he was on the ice at even strength (matched from the NHL's shift charts). That score is then compared with every game played at the same position this season. Where it lands sets the rating.

    During a live game, ratings update every minute and are compared with full games, so they start low and climb as players build their night.

    What the advanced stats mean

    • Expected goals (xG) is the chance an average shooter scores from that shot, based on distance, angle, shot type, rebounds, rush chances, and the power play. This site uses its own model built from the NHL's shot coordinates. Empty-net shots are left out of a player's expected goals because empty-net goals have their own weight.
    • High-danger chances are unblocked shots from within 25 feet and at most 45 degrees off center.
    • On-ice, even strength counts what happened while the player was on the ice with both teams at full strength (including 4-on-4 and overtime 3-on-3). This is how defensive play and playmaking show up even without points. During live games the NHL's shift data can lag a few minutes behind.
    • Goals saved above expected (GSAx) is the expected-goal total of unblocked shots a goalie faced minus the goals he allowed, excluding empty-net shots.
    • Expected assists are not included. The NHL feed doesn't record passes, so there is no reliable way to know who set up a shot that didn't go in. On-ice expected goals for is the closest substitute.
    • Shootouts don't count toward any rating. Skaters under 5 minutes of ice time get no rating. Goalies are graded against goalies who played at least 20 minutes.
    Data from the NHL's public box scores, play-by-play, shift charts, and time-on-ice reports. Ratings and expected goals are this site's own models, not official NHL statistics.
